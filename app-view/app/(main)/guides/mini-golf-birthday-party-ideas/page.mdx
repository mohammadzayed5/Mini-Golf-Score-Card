# Mini Golf Birthday Party Ideas (From a Parent Who Has Run Them)

For a pack of 8 to 12 year olds, run mini golf in groups of four, plan for about 90 minutes on the course plus 45 for food, and hand out prizes for things other than lowest score. That is the whole trick. Everything below is about not getting stuck behind a slow group and not sending anyone home upset.

## How many kids, and how to group them

Four per group is the ceiling. Five kids will stand on the tee mat arguing about who goes next and the group behind you will start putting into your ankles. If your guest list is nine kids, run two groups of four and one group of three, not three groups of three (an odd group of three finishes way too fast and comes back begging for snacks while the others are on hole 5).

A few things that help when you split them up:

- Put the birthday kid with their two closest friends, not the whole crew. They will want to focus on the round with the people they picked. {/* FOUNDER-CONFIRM: verify this claim */}
- Split known rivals across groups. The kid who insists Dad took a 7 not a 5 is the same kid who will contest every score.
- Send one adult with each group. Not to score, to referee. More on that below.
- Give each group a color or a team name at the start. It makes the leaderboard reveal at the end feel like something.

If it is an older group (11 to 12), you can push to groups of five if the course is quiet on a weekday afternoon. On a Saturday, do not try it.

## Timing the day so nothing stalls

Most 18-hole courses take a group of four kids somewhere between 75 and 100 minutes. Plan for 90 and you will be fine. Here is a schedule that has held up:

- **0:00 to 0:15**: Arrival, bathroom, hand out putters and colored balls. Kids will lose balls. Colored balls make it obvious whose ball went in the water feature on hole 6.
- **0:15 to 1:45**: Play the round. Groups tee off 5 minutes apart so they never pile up.
- **1:45 to 2:15**: Food.
- **2:15 to 2:30**: Prizes, cake, goodie bags.
- **2:30**: Pickup.

Two hours thirty is the sweet spot for this age; three hours in and the younger ones start melting.

## Keeping the round moving

The single biggest reason a kids' round stalls is the 6-stroke rule not being enforced. Most courses have a max of 6 or 7 per hole. Nobody tells the kids. So one kid takes 14 whacks on the windmill hole while three groups back up behind them.

Tell every group before they start: **max 6 strokes. If you hit 6 and it is not in, pick up, write down 6, move on.** Have the adult with each group say it out loud on hole 1 and hole 2. After that it sticks.

Other things that speed the round:

- Kids should tee off in the order they finished the last hole (worst score goes first on the next hole). It cuts arguments.
- Put clubs down between shots. Kids swinging putters near other kids is how somebody ends up with a bump on their forehead.
- Skip a hole if you're stuck behind a slow group for more than five minutes. Come back to it if the course loops.

## Food that survives the car

You are eating outside on a picnic table or the trunk of a car. Whatever you bring has to survive 40 minutes in a warm bag while the kids finish the round. What works:

- Pizza picked up on the way. Order it timed for the food break, not the start of the party.
- Sandwich platter from a grocery store. Cheap, no reheating.
- Fruit that does not brown fast (grapes, watermelon chunks, strawberries).
- Individual drinks with lids. Open cups on a picnic table with 10 kids is a disaster.

Skip: anything that needs a fork and knife, anything with a sauce that separates, birthday cakes with real buttercream in July. Ice cream cake in a cooler with dry ice is the move.

## Prizes that do not embarrass the last-place kid

Do not hand out a "lowest score" trophy. The kid who came last already knows they came last, and they are 8. Instead, give out four or five small prizes across categories so almost every kid wins something:

- **Best trick shot** (the bank shot on hole 12 that went in)
- **Best cheerleader** (the kid who lost their mind celebrating other kids' hole-in-ones)
- **Closest to par** (rewards steady play, which is a different skill than raw talent)
- **Longest putt made**
- **Best sportsmanship** (real one, the adult refs pick this)

Prizes can be $3 items. Golf-themed pencils, mini golf ball keychains, candy. The recognition matters more than what is in the bag.

For the actual leaderboard, announce the top three by group color instead of by name if you want to soften it further. Kids can figure out who won without you shouting the last kid's total across the parking lot.

## Scoring without paper

The tiny pencil the course hands you is going to snap. The paper card is going to blow off the picnic table on hole 7. If two groups are playing, you now have two cards to keep track of and one of them is definitely wrong.

Put one adult per group in charge of scoring on their phone. Enter every kid at the start, tap plus after each hole, done. When groups finish, compare on one screen instead of squinting at two damp paper cards.
