# How to Build a Backyard Mini Golf Course

Build a nine-hole backyard mini golf course with pool noodles, PVC, and stuff already in the garage. You need a weekend, about $40 if you buy fresh materials (less if you raid the shed), and a yard big enough to walk in a rough loop. Nine holes is the sweet spot: enough variety to feel like a real course, few enough that an 8-year-old with an oversized putter still wants to play the last one.

## Pick a hole count that fits your yard

Nine holes is the target because it maps to the way people already think about mini golf. If your yard is very small (think patio plus a strip of grass), do six and play the loop twice. If you have a bigger space with a side yard, eighteen is doable but honestly overkill for a backyard, kids get bored around hole twelve.

Rough space budget per hole:

- Straight putt: 6 to 10 feet long, 2 feet wide
- Dogleg or bank shot: 8 by 8 feet
- Ramp or elevated finish: 10 feet long, needs a flat runup

Walk the yard first. Count your trees, the AC unit, the dog's favorite spot. Those are free obstacles, use them.

## Cheap obstacles that hold up

Every hole needs a bit of personality or it's just putting on the lawn. Here's what works and survives being stepped on by a barefoot cousin.

**Pool noodles.** Cut them lengthwise with a bread knife, staple or landscape-stake them into the grass, and you have soft rails that curve into any shape you want. Great for banking shots off. A $1 noodle makes about 4 feet of rail.

**PVC pipe (1/2 inch).** Two lengths staked parallel make a straight fairway. Bend gently for a slow curve. Elbows and tees let you make gates the ball has to thread through.

**5-gallon buckets.** Cut a rectangle out of the side, flip it upside down, and you have a tunnel. Weight the top with a brick so it doesn't scoot when the ball hits it.

**Plywood scraps.** A 2 by 2 foot piece of quarter-inch plywood propped on a paver becomes a ramp. Add a second one leaning the other way for a jump.

**Cardboard boxes.** Windmill-adjacent stuff. Cut a mouse-hole shape, tape the box to a stake, and the kids have a target to aim at. Won't survive rain, but by then the tournament's over.

**Cups sunk in the ground.** For the actual hole, dig a spot for a plastic cup so the rim sits flush with the grass. Yogurt containers work if you don't have anything else. If you can't dig (renters, wife will kill you, patio), lay a cup on its side against a small ramp of dirt and call that the hole.

## Layout tips that keep it fun

The order matters more than the individual holes. A few rules that make the round feel designed instead of thrown together:

1. **Start easy.** Hole 1 should be a straight putt that a 5-year-old can par. You want everybody feeling good.
2. **Alternate difficulty.** Don't stack three ramps in a row. Boring hole, tricky hole, boring hole, tricky hole. Peaks and valleys.
3. **End memorable.** Hole 9 should have a story to it. A long tunnel, a bank shot off the fence, something people will argue about later.
4. **Flow the walking.** Each hole's finish should be near the next hole's tee. Nobody wants to march across the whole yard between shots.
5. **Watch the sun.** If half your holes are in blinding afternoon sun, move them or plan the round for after 5pm.

Draw the loop on paper before you build anything. Napkin sketch is fine. You'll change your mind twice while you're staking things down, and that's normal.

## Setting par

Par 2 for straight putts, par 3 for anything with a turn or a ramp, par 4 only for the one showoff hole with three obstacles. Total par for nine should land around 24 to 27. If Grandpa is shooting 40, your pars are too tight. If a 10-year-old is shooting 20, they're too loose.

## Scoring without the tiny pencil

Here's where the paper scorecard falls apart. Literally. It gets wet, blows off the picnic table, the pencil goes missing, someone erases their 7 and writes a 4, and by hole 6 nobody remembers whose turn it is.

Use your phone. Open the app, tap Start New Game, name the course whatever you called your yard tournament ("Backyard Open 2026" is fine), set it to 9 holes, and add every player including the neighbor who wandered over. Tap plus or minus per hole. Pull up the leaderboard between holes when someone claims they're winning. When the round ends, the app throws confetti at the winner and adds a tally to their running win count, which matters more than you'd think when the same kid wins three weekends in a row.

## Weekend build schedule

If you want a plan:

- **Saturday morning:** Walk the yard, sketch the loop, list what you need. Hardware store run.
- **Saturday afternoon:** Sink the cups, stake the pool noodle rails, cut the bucket tunnels.
- **Sunday morning:** Build the two or three "feature" holes (ramp, dogleg, tunnel).
- **Sunday afternoon:** Test round with the family. Adjust pars. Fix the hole where the ball keeps rolling into the flower bed.

That's it. You now have a course. Try not to leave the plywood ramp out in the rain.
