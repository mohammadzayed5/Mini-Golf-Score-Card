# Mini Golf Scoring Cheat Sheet

Par on a mini golf hole is almost always 2, the max strokes you write down is 6 (some courses use 7), and the lowest total score wins. That's the whole game in one line. Everything below is the stuff you'll reach for when the pencil is tiny, the wind is picking up, and your dad is arguing that his 7 was really a 5.

## The one-screen summary

Keep this in your head and you'll never fumble a scorecard again.

- **Par per hole:** 2 (some tricky holes are printed as par 3)
- **Hole-in-one (ace):** 1
- **Birdie:** 1 under par (so 1 on a par 2)
- **Bogey:** 1 over par (3 on a par 2)
- **Double bogey:** 2 over par (4 on a par 2)
- **Triple bogey:** 3 over par (5 on a par 2)
- **Max strokes per hole:** 6 (sometimes 7, check the sign at hole 1)
- **Course par (18 holes):** usually 36 to 40
- **Winner:** lowest total across all holes

If a hole is printed as par 3 (long fairway, big loop, that one with the windmill), a birdie is 2 and bogey is 4. Same idea, just shifted up by one.

## The stroke ladder on a par 2

Nine out of ten holes you play will be par 2, so memorize this row and you're set:

| Strokes | Name |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Hole-in-one |
| 2 | Par |
| 3 | Bogey |
| 4 | Double bogey |
| 5 | Triple bogey |
| 6 | Max (write it down and move on) |

The max is not a rule the golf gods handed down. It's a courtesy. It keeps the group behind you from watching your 8-year-old take 14 swings with an oversized putter. If you hit the printed limit, you pick up the ball, write the max, and walk to the next hole.

## What counts as a stroke

This is where 90% of the arguments start.

- Every intentional swing at the ball counts, even if you whiff and the ball doesn't move.
- Practice swings do not count, as long as you clearly stopped short of the ball.
- If the ball bounces off the wall and comes back, that's still one stroke, not two.
- If the ball goes off the green (into the rocks, the pond, the neighbor's flowerbed), you take a one-stroke penalty and place it back where it went out.
- Nudging the ball with your foot to "line it up" is a stroke if anyone in the group saw it. And they saw it.

The cleanest way to keep this straight: count out loud as you swing. "One. Two. Three." Nobody can revise history if the whole group heard the count.

## Reading the scorecard row by row

A standard 18-hole card has hole numbers across the top, player names down the side, and a total column on the right. Fill it in like this:

1. After each hole, write the stroke count in that player's box.
2. Update the running total in your head or in the margin. It's the sum of every box so far.
3. At hole 9, add a subtotal. This is the "front nine." It doubles as a check when you get to 18.
4. At hole 18, add the back nine, then sum front + back. That's the final score.

If a scorecard blows away in a beach breeze (this happens), the running total is what saves you. You can rebuild the card from memory if you know where each player stood at hole 9 and hole 18.

## Breaking a tie

Ties happen more than you'd think, especially in a family of four playing a short course. Here's the order most casual groups use:

1. **Sudden death.** Play hole 1 again. Lowest score wins. If still tied, play hole 2. Repeat.
2. **Fewest bogeys.** Count how many holes each tied player finished over par. Fewer bogeys wins.
3. **Most aces.** Count hole-in-ones. Most aces wins.
4. **Back nine.** Whoever scored lower on holes 10 to 18 wins.

Pick one before the round starts and stick with it. Arguing about tiebreaker rules after the fact is how mini golf trips end early.

## Quick conversions (par 2 vs par 3 holes)

Some courses mix in a few par 3 holes for the longer stretches. Same names, different numbers.

| Score | Par 2 hole | Par 3 hole |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ace | 1 | 1 |
| Birdie | (n/a) | 2 |
| Par | 2 | 3 |
| Bogey | 3 | 4 |
| Double bogey | 4 | 5 |
| Triple bogey | 5 | 6 |

There's no "eagle" on a par 2, because you can't go two under 2 without inventing a new rule. An ace on a par 2 is already a birdie. On a par 3, an ace is an eagle.
